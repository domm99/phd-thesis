\begin{abstract}
\Glspl{CAS} comprise distributed devices or agents whose local interactions 
 give rise to emergent collective behavior that cannot be attributed to any component in isolation.
%
Such behavior can be engineered through explicit coordination rules, 
 expressed for instance through macroprogramming, 
 or through learning mechanisms that derive models and decision policies from distributed data and experience.
%
This thesis brings these two perspectives together 
 to investigate how cooperative learning 
 and decision making can be engineered as integral parts of a \gls{CAS}.
%
This requires addressing conditions that conventional learning methods often abstract away, 
 including heterogeneous and distributed data, local communication, changing populations, 
 and limited computational resources.

Three complementary lines of research address these challenges.
%
First, 
 field-based coordination enables devices to form and reorganize federated learning groups 
 according to spatial proximity and model compatibility, without centralizing raw data, 
 relying on a permanent server, or fixing the number of groups in advance.
%
This approach is extended with model sparsification 
 and supported by a benchmark for spatially structured data heterogeneity.
%
Preliminary extensions further investigate task-independent collaborator discovery 
 and continual adaptation under mobility.
 %
Second, 
 decentralized reinforcement learning is studied through neighbor-based training strategies 
 and graph-based policies that can operate across different swarm sizes.
%
Third, 
 a graph-based offloading approach combines local device information with aggregate estimates of collective conditions 
 to place application components across edge and cloud resources.

Simulation results show that self-organizing federations learn models specialized to spatially heterogeneous data, 
 outperform single-model baselines under strong label skew, match a clustered baseline provided with the correct number of clusters, 
 and reorganize after failures and device movement.
%
Moderate sparsification preserves these properties, 
 whereas aggressive pruning destabilizes federation formation.
%
In decentralized reinforcement learning, 
 exchanging experiences or parameters with neighboring agents narrows the performance gap with centralized training,
 while graph-based policies trained with ten agents remain functional in swarms nearly three times larger, 
 although their efficiency progressively declines.
%
Finally, 
 incorporating collective density information enables agents to differentiate offloading decisions 
 between congested and sparse areas, a behavior that does not emerge when such information is removed.
%
Together, 
 these findings show that explicitly representing and organizing the structure of the collective 
 can support learning and decentralized decision making under the operating conditions of \glspl{CAS}.

\sloppypar
    \textbf{Keywords} -- \emph{\glsentryfull{CAS}, \gls{CFL}, \gls{MARL}, \gls{MP}}

\end{abstract}
