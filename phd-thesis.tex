\begin{abstract}

Networks of sensors, mobile devices, and robots can cooperate
to provide services and solve problems beyond the capabilities of individual components.
%
In \glspl{CAS}, this cooperation relies on local interactions
through which collective behavior emerges and adapts to changing conditions.
%
Engineering learning in these systems poses several challenges:
observations are distributed and heterogeneous,
communication is local,
populations and environments change,
and computation competes for limited resources.
%
These characteristics complicate both the organization of learning
and the execution of decisions that must account for their effects on the collective.

This thesis investigates methods and tools for engineering cooperative learning
and decentralized decision making in \glspl{CAS}.
%
The approach combines machine learning with distributed mechanisms for collective coordination,
including macroprogramming techniques such as aggregate computing.
%
These mechanisms organize collaboration and information exchange among devices,
while learning derives models and decision policies from distributed data and experience.

To support collaborative learning without centralizing raw observations,
the thesis develops self-organizing federated learning methods.
%
Field-based coordination enables devices to form and reorganize learning groups
according to spatial proximity and model compatibility,
without a permanent server or a predefined number of groups.
%
Simulation results show that these methods support models specialized to heterogeneous data
and sustain collaboration under failures and mobility.

Cooperative decision making is investigated through decentralized reinforcement learning,
allowing agents to learn from interaction with their environment.
%
Neighbor-based exchanges improve decentralized training,
while graph-based policies allow learned behaviors to operate across different collective sizes,
although performance remains sensitive to changes in scale.
%
Resource constraints are addressed through a graph-based learning approach
for placing application components across edge and cloud resources.
%
By combining local observations with aggregate estimates of collective conditions,
the resulting policies adapt placement decisions to mobility and shared infrastructure conditions,
balancing energy consumption, infrastructure costs, and server load.

The engineering contribution also includes software tools
for integrating aggregate programs with learning algorithms,
constructing spatially structured benchmarks,
conducting simulation experiments,
and executing collective programs on physical robots.
%
Together, these contributions provide methods, practical support, and empirical evidence
for engineering learning and decision making around the interactions,
organization, and resource constraints of collective systems.

\end{abstract}
