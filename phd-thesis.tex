\begin{abstract}

In \glspl{CAS}, networks of sensors, mobile devices, and robots
cooperate through local interactions,
from which collective behavior emerges and adapts to changing conditions.
%
Engineering learning and autonomy in these systems poses several challenges:
observations are distributed, heterogeneous, and often sensitive,
communication is local,
populations and environments change,
and computation competes for limited resources.

This thesis investigates methods and tools for engineering cooperative learning
and decentralized decision making in many-agent \glspl{CAS},
combining machine learning with distributed coordination mechanisms
such as aggregate computing.

Self-organizing federated learning methods support collaborative learning
without centralizing raw observations.
%
Field-based coordination lets devices form and reorganize learning groups
according to spatial proximity and model compatibility,
without a permanent server or a predefined number of groups.
%
Under spatially heterogeneous data,
the resulting regional models outperform single-global-model approaches,
and groups reorganize after leader failures and device movement.
%
Preliminary results show that local replay mitigates forgetting of previously visited regions,
while model sparsification reduces computational costs
but, beyond a dataset-dependent threshold, disrupts group formation.

\sloppypar
Cooperative decision making is investigated through decentralized multi-agent reinforcement learning.
%
Exchanging experiences or model information within bounded neighborhoods
brings decentralized training close to centralized performance,
while graph-based policies remain functional without retraining in larger collectives,
although their efficiency degrades as population size departs from training.

For resource-constrained deployment,
graph-based reinforcement learning places application components across edge and cloud resources.
%
By combining local observations with aggregate estimates of collective conditions,
policies adapt to device density and mobility,
balancing energy consumption, infrastructure costs, and server load;
without this collective information, devices converge toward uniform strategies.

% Field-based coordination is also applied to distributed state estimation,
% organizing cooperative particle filtering and reassigning the fusion role after failures.
%
Software tools complete the contribution,
supporting aggregate programs integrated with learning,
spatially structured benchmarks, simulation experiments,
and execution on physical robots.

Across these settings,
making collective structure explicit
(learning groups, interaction neighborhoods, and shared infrastructure conditions)
improves learning and decentralized decisions
without requiring any device to observe or control the entire system.
%
% Each mechanism is evaluated separately in simulation;
% integrating them into a single architecture is left to future work.

\sloppypar
    \textbf{Keywords} -- \emph{\glsentryfull{CAS}, \gls{CFL}, \gls{MARL}, \gls{MP}}

\end{abstract}
