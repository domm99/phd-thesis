\begin{abstract}	
\glspl{CAS} comprise devices whose local observations 
 and actions jointly shape the behavior of the collective. 
%
Enabling them to learn and act together is challenging when data are distributed and heterogeneous, 
 communication is local, populations change, and computational resources are limited. 
%
Conventional learning methods often assume centralized coordination, 
 fixed populations, or a shared data distribution. 
% 
\emph{This thesis investigates cooperative learning 
 and decision making under the conditions of a \gls{CAS}.}

Three complementary lines of research address different aspects of this problem. 
%
First, 
 field-based coordination enables devices to form and reorganize federated learning groups 
 according to spatial proximity and model compatibility, 
 without pooling raw data or relying on a permanent central server. 
% 
Preliminary work explores random network distillation to identify compatible collaborators independently of the task model, 
 and continual learning to retain knowledge as devices move between regions. 
% 
Second, 
 exchanges among neighbors improve decentralized reinforcement learning, 
 while graph-based policies support execution in swarms larger than those seen during training, 
 albeit with declining efficiency. 
% 
Third, 
 a graph-based offloading approach combines local device information with aggregate estimates 
  of shared conditions to guide application placement across edge and cloud resources.

In simulated evaluations, 
 self-organizing federations support models specialized to spatially heterogeneous data 
 and reorganize after changes in membership or position. 
% 
Neighbor exchanges improve learning over independent agents, 
 while graph-based policies remain functional in larger swarms, 
 though their efficiency declines. 
% 
Collective density information also enables offloading decisions 
 that differ between congested and sparse areas. 
% 
These findings show the value of collective structure across the three settings 
 and delineate the remaining challenges of adapting to changing populations, distributions, and resources.
\end{abstract}
